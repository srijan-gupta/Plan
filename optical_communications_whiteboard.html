Optical communications: research fit whiteboard

Working question: where can a physics or signal-processing problem make numerical modelling and performance-critical implementation part of the research, with ownership of the investigation rather than routine benchmark runs?

29 September 2026 · Research map, not a claim of available funded PhD openings. The database retains the detailed person-level ratings.

Four connected routes

Closest intersection

Real-time coherent receiver DSP

Problem: Can a physically justified receiver meet throughput, latency and power constraints? Link impairments → estimation/equalization → GPU/CPU/FPGA implementation → measured BER and throughput → investigate model–hardware discrepancies.

Useful skills: C/C++, SIMD/GPU, FFT, memory traffic, pipelining, precision, profiling. Published GPU receiver demonstrations make this concrete; student-level kernel ownership still needs checking.

Physics and mathematics

Nonlinear fiber propagation and equalization

Problem: Dispersion and nonlinearities limit reach/capacity, while digital backpropagation can cost too much. NLSE/Manakov model → split-step or inverse-scattering computation → reduced-complexity algorithm → validate on realistic links.

Risk: A thesis can be mostly mathematical DSP with little low-level code. Ask who writes and optimizes the simulator and receiver.

Computational science

Fast channel and system simulation

Problem: Accurate sweeps over links, fibers, power, noise and formats may be too slow for design or inference. Numerical fidelity, fast solvers, parallelism and measurement correlation can be research constraints.

Evidence gap: A lab using a commercial simulator does not imply a student develops performance-critical scientific software.

Adjacent to optical I/O

Computational photonics and inverse design

Problem: Maxwell-scale device simulations and repeated adjoint solves make photonic optimization costly. FDTD/FEM → differentiable/adjoint model → geometry → fabrication/measurement → discrepancy → revision.

Risk: This may center a device or fabrication thesis rather than link architecture. Solver/kernel development must be a real student role.

What would make a thesis worth five years?

A consequential link or device bottleneck; an uncertain physical mechanism or computational limit; freedom to build models and choose tests; an iterative model → probe → anomaly → root cause → refinement loop; and a result useful even if the original hypothesis fails. Performance work may be an advantage, a substantial thesis component, or the research question itself. Do not require assembly-level work in every project.

Questions for papers, students and PIs

  1. Which concrete limitation are you trying to overcome, and which part is still poorly understood?
  2. Who built the physical model, numerical solver and performance-critical implementation in the last two student projects?
  3. Was complexity measured as asymptotic operations, real throughput/latency/power, or both? On which hardware?
  4. What did students do when simulations disagreed with transmission measurements?
  5. How much of the thesis is problem formulation versus prescribed sweeps, fabrication waiting, or testbed maintenance?
  6. Can the student own a long investigation and retain publishable physics or transferable R&D value if the main design fails?
