The clearest current formulation is:

> **Owner of a difficult real problem who is willing and able to descend all the way into fundamental research when the problem demands it.**

This is a preferred identity, not a claim that every past job already afforded that ownership. A real problem can be scientific or technological. Fundamental understanding is worthwhile when the unresolved question itself matters or when it enables the larger problem to move forward.

### Important distinctions

- **Problem-led research:** important problem → attempted solution → exposed blocker → targeted investigation → knowledge/capability → feed back into the problem. Selecting an unexplored literature corner for novelty alone is not a motivating default.
- **Ownership:** code, experiments or diagnostics can be personally executed without ownership of the scientific reasoning. These forms of ownership must be assessed separately.
- **Observability:** improving tools and instrumentation can be part of solving the problem. Infrastructure becomes valuable through the hypotheses, measurements and decisions it enables.
- **Method freedom:** modelling, theory, experiments, statistics, algorithms, C/C++, hardware and unfamiliar physics are instruments. No preferred recipe should override the problem.
- **Capability versus purpose:** being good at low-level programming does not alone make a project meaningful. Conversely, performance work can be valuable when simulation cost blocks scientific progress.
- **Meaning versus feedback:** a measurable objective can still be an unimportant one; an important mission can still offer poor daily ownership. Both layers matter.

### Decision Framework (current)

The [operational research filter](../research_decision_framework.md) holds the detailed screening procedure. Its governing priorities are important problems, long-horizon ownership, objective feedback, deep scientific/system understanding, external evidence and an enabling environment.

Five years should remain worthwhile even if the central hypothesis fails by resolving an important unknown, clarifying/removing a technology blocker, or building deep transferable R&D capability. Novelty and prestige alone do not establish value.

Physics and optimization have gained emphasis. Applied mathematics, inference, algorithms, measurements and HPC are useful fit signals, subordinate to the problem and ownership. Maintain credible technical capability, including C/C++ and performance work where useful, without requiring every project to contain all these methods.

The preferred loop is model/simulator → design probe or experiment → study results → diagnose discrepancy/mechanism → refine → probe again. This is a strong preference, not a mandatory ritual.

Prestige, maximum salary and external validation remain secondary. Pay downside can be acceptable for worthwhile experimental physics; practical career and life constraints still need to be tested before concrete commitments.

### Current Hypotheses

**Best current explanation:** the strongest work structure is sustained ownership of an important physical or systems problem, with authority to investigate blockers, build models/measurement capability and use evidence to decide the next step.

Confidence: High in the structure as a preference; Medium in which role/domain delivers it in practice.

The July v2.1 hypothesis that industrial R&D was the strongest environment is now one live route, not the established winner. Experimental research and computational physical modelling also survive. This widening follows the ownership/method-freedom evidence, not a rejection of industrial R&D.
