# Evidence register

Updated 1 October 2026. Evidence below comes from the preserved July documents and dated Field conversations available in project context. Conversation titles and dates are retrieval locators, not fabricated public links. Confidence describes the interpretation, not source verification of external opportunities. No new thesis or laboratory audit was performed for this identity update.

## July baseline

### E01 — Depth and fragmented cognition

**Source:** [July v2.1 baseline](archive/Intellectual_Identity_and_Life_Direction_v2.1.md), Current Model.

**Situation / observation:** the baseline records enjoyment of thinking, deep understanding, one coherent long-term problem and dissatisfaction with fragmented cognition at Qualcomm.

**Interpretation:** dislike of fragmented work does not imply dislike of engineering. **Confidence:** High as a repeated preference; the older baseline summarizes experiences rather than reproducing primary conversations.

**Framework / decision implication:** assess context switching, investigation timescale and accumulated ownership in research and industry. **Competing explanation / revision trigger:** dissatisfaction may partly reflect a particular team; sustained coherent engineering work could discriminate this.

### E02 — Mathematical models, emergence and wonder

**Source:** July v2.1, intellectual identity and statistical-physics evidence highlight.

**Observation:** mathematical modelling, emergence and deep systems were motivating; wonder and aesthetics mattered.

**Interpretation:** attraction to explanatory models. **Confidence:** Medium–High. **Implication:** preserve mathematical/physical depth without treating all mathematical work as equally attractive. **Revision trigger:** real modelling work proves consistently draining or domain context dominates method preference.

### E03 — Knowledge becoming capability

**Source:** July v2.1, ASML / Blue LED evidence highlight.

**Observation:** transforming understanding into capability increased motivation.

**Interpretation:** contribution includes scientific knowledge that changes what can be built or achieved. **Confidence:** Medium–High. **Implication:** trace the connection between knowledge and a valued outcome. **Alternative:** inspirational examples may overstate daily motivation; test in actual work.

### E04 — Beauty alone and the July environment hypothesis

**Source:** July v2.1, optics evidence and Current Hypotheses.

**Observation:** aesthetic attraction alone was insufficient; industrial R&D on deep systems/physics problems was then the strongest hypothesis, with Medium confidence.

**Interpretation / implication:** separate domain attraction, work structure and occupation. Later evidence broadens the viable environments. **Confidence:** Medium. **Revision trigger:** comparable sustained experiences establish a stronger environment preference.

## September–October evidence

### E05 — Investigation loop and problem-led research

**Source:** Field discussions, 14 September 2026 (preferred investigation loop), 25 September (value even if wrong), 27 September (problem → blocking gap → research → capability); carried into [the operational filter](../research_decision_framework.md).

**Observation:** requested simulator/model → experiment → results → debug → mechanism → refine → probe; required worthwhile outcomes even after hypothesis failure; made problem-led inquiry a standing filter.

**Interpretation:** sustained scientific problem-solving matters more than a prescribed method. **Confidence:** High as repeated explicit preference. **Implication:** screen important problems and decision-changing evidence; do not select novelty first. **Revision trigger:** actual investigations expose a different satisfying pattern.

### E06 — Performance-critical physical simulation

**Source:** “Disassembly Optimization Uses,” “New HPC Findings,” “Evaluate optical HPC fit” (29 September); “Physics Simulation Frontiers,” Aalto continuation (30 September).

**Observation:** wanted physical problem-solving, applied maths and HPC; saw simulation as a place where theories, numerics and low-level performance can interact; specifically valued objective comparison.

**Interpretation:** physical modelling can integrate existing C/C++/optimization capability into a meaningful scientific loop. **Confidence:** High for expressed interest, Medium for sustained role fit. **Implication:** retain computational physical modelling independent of Aalto; distinguish physics, numerics, code, performance and validation ownership. **Alternative / revision trigger:** enthusiasm may be driven by familiar implementation skills; a real project should test whether physics/numerics remain motivating.

### E07 — Contribution over decades

**Source:** “Meaningful work psychology,” 30 September 2026.

**Observation:** wanted purpose through work and a visible link from daily effort to advancing humanity over 2–3 decades; named energy, extraterrestrial technology and fundamental physics as examples.

**Interpretation:** a long-term mission is a distinct motivation axis. **Confidence:** High for aspiration, Medium for how it translates into daily choices. **Implication:** ask what human/scientific capability advances and how the project contributes. **Alternative / revision trigger:** the mission might mainly supply framing; compare daily engagement across equally deep problems with different missions.

### E08 — A worthwhile unknown, even if the hypothesis fails

**Source:** standing five-year value filter, 25 September 2026, and 30 September contribution discussion.

**Observation:** accepted important unknowns, technology-blocking physics and deep transferable R&D capability as valuable outcomes; experimental fundamental physics remained attractive even with pay downside.

**Interpretation:** useful outcome is broader than immediate commercialization. **Confidence:** High for stated criterion. **Implication:** do not reject fundamental work merely for lack of a near-term product. **Revision trigger:** actual costs and outcomes change the acceptable trade-off.

### E09 — Own the reasoning, not just execution

**Source:** “CERN Posting Clarified,” 1 October 2026; [student-ownership test](../research_decision_framework.md); repeated preference for depth/autonomy.

**Observation:** explicitly endorsed “Owner of a difficult real problem who is willing and able to descend all the way into fundamental research when the problem demands it.” Requested problem-solving groups and rejected gap-hunting for its own sake.

**Interpretation:** scientific ownership includes choosing and interpreting the next investigation. **Confidence:** High as an explicit clarification supported by earlier preferences. **Implication:** examine causal student trajectories and growing reasoning ownership. **Alternative / revision trigger:** full decision responsibility may feel different from imagined autonomy; supervised research experience is needed.

### E10 — Observability before optimization

**Source:** “Learn NEON Disassembly,” 29 September; “Debugger importance explained,” 1 October 2026.

**Observation:** wanted visibility into register/cache/instruction bottlenecks rather than guessing from noisy performance differences. Later described wanting relevant state easily accessible, rich diagnostics and trustworthy measurements before efficient optimization.

**Interpretation:** capability building and mechanism identification are recurring instincts. **Confidence:** High for preference and continuity with current engineering behaviour. **Implication:** evaluate whether a role permits designing probes and improving observability; diagnostics should change decisions. **Alternative / revision trigger:** instrumentation could be an uncertainty-reduction habit that delays action; test whether each diagnostic is worth its cost.

### E11 — Reduce friction and investigate tool limits

**Source:** “Debugger importance explained,” 1 October 2026.

**Observation:** wanted idea testing to become extremely easy, with little inertia; described descending into deeper tool layers to discover what is possible, measurable and best measured.

**Interpretation:** improving the investigation environment is part of the craft, not incidental convenience. **Confidence:** High for the direct statement; generalization beyond engineering remains Medium. **Implication:** prefer environments where tooling can support faster, better discriminating investigations. **Revision trigger:** research work shows tooling interest outweighs interest in the scientific result.

### E12 — Explicit rejection criteria and revisable decisions

**Source:** “Apply Database Pruning,” 1 October 2026.

**Observation:** requested a growing record of candidates that aligned on many axes but failed a named filter, retaining the rejection criteria.

**Interpretation:** wants reconstructable decisions rather than unexplained rankings. **Confidence:** High for the requested process; it is not evidence of a new personality trait. **Implication:** preserve aligned aspects, failure scope, uncertainty and reconsideration conditions. **Revision trigger:** better evidence can reverse a candidate disposition without erasing history.

### E13 — AI and durable capability remain unsettled

**Source:** “HPC Automation Risk,” 29 September; “Assess Computational Physics Risk,” 30 September 2026.

**Observation:** questioned whether coding, software design and domain knowledge provide lasting protection from AI automation; wanted to retain development capability and tool fluency.

**Interpretation:** durable expertise and outcome responsibility matter, but no immunity claim is supported. **Confidence:** High for the concern, Low for future labour-market conclusions. **Implication:** assess actual learning/ownership opportunities; keep forecasts separate from identity evidence. **Revision trigger:** observed changes in scientific workflows and hiring, not reassuring labels.

## Evidence limits

Most recent entries are direct expressed preferences and discussion-based interpretations. They are not equivalent to multi-year research experience. No unverified CV performance numbers, laboratory claims or salary estimates are used to strengthen the identity model. Confidence should increase through independent experiences and discriminating tests, not repetition of the same discussion.
