Fusion research: where your fit is strongest

Two directions worth testing through actual papers, student theses and conversations with supervisors. The shared goal is an applied physics thesis where experiment and model challenge each other, and the answer changes a consequential prediction or design choice.

Your bridge

Engineering Physics and quantum/statistical coursework → THz pump–probe spectroscopy and physical parameter extraction → optics and signal processing → camera sensor chains, C/C++, embedded debugging. The valuable combination is measurement → inference → physical model → discrepancy → better experiment. Plasma physics and numerical methods are the skills to acquire in the PhD.

A. Magnetic fusion: edge, exhaust and transport

Highest direct reactor bottleneck

Question: How do heat, particles and impurities leave a confined plasma while preserving core performance and keeping the wall alive? Look for detachment, radiation, neutrals, turbulence and plasma–wall interaction studied through diagnostics and modelling.

How to choose a thesis

  1. Name the unresolved mechanism and its consequence for a reactor or predictive model.
  2. Ask which measurements, inference code, simulation and experiment design you would own.
  3. Read three student papers and one thesis from each leading group. Follow a discrepancy from measurement to a revised physical explanation.
  4. Ask what durable capability remains if the initial hypothesis fails.

Current hypothesis: DIFFER is the strongest near-term skill intersection; MAGPIE is the strongest test of whether extreme plasma physics itself is the more compelling long-term subject. These are diligence priorities, not admissions predictions.
